# Upgrade evidence pack

Use this before a purchase recommendation. It turns a vague “should I upgrade?” request into evidence that can be reviewed later.

## Required evidence for a functional upgrade

- active device and current configuration report;
- primary pain or target workload;
- current resolution, quality, frame cap, or production settings;
- measured FPS/frame time, render/compile time, capacity or other current result;
- CPU/GPU utilization, memory/VRAM pressure, temperatures, power, and limit reasons when relevant;
- non-purchase attempts already tested;
- desired result and hard budget/region/currency if the user wants purchase analysis.

Unknown optional evidence remains unknown; do not invent it.

## Preference and future-project purchases

If the user explicitly wants an aesthetic, collection, novelty, gift, or planning purchase, do not invent a bottleneck to justify it. Use a separate evidence set:

- the actual goal and whether it is preference, capability, or performance;
- hard budget, region, and currency if relevant;
- physical fit, power, platform, and software compatibility;
- return, warranty, and delivery conditions;
- opportunity cost and what the purchase prevents them from doing later;
- for future projects, the assumed future workload and uncertainty of that forecast.

Label preference value as preference value. It is neither a performance gain nor a reason to claim that existing hardware is inadequate.

## Preparation report for preference or planning

Record:

```text
Purchase goal and why it matters:
Desired experience or capability:
Active device and compatibility:
Budget/region/currency:
Current cash cost and retained/sold/gifted disposition:
Return, warranty, delivery, and physical-fit conditions:
Alternative non-purchase options:
Opportunity cost and uncertainty:
Remaining evidence needed:
```

For a preference or future-project purchase, do not fabricate a Pain,
performance bottleneck, or measured-result requirement. Use a claimed future
workload only as a forecast and state its uncertainty.

## Decision order

1. keep the current hardware if it meets the goal;
2. tune settings or configuration;
3. repair a known faulty part;
4. replace one component when it addresses the measured bottleneck;
5. rebuild the platform only for a material multi-workload benefit;
6. wait if price, drivers, firmware, or standards are immature.

Do not call this a purchase recommendation until the decision framework and current market evidence support one.

## Follow-up

After the user tests a non-purchase option or supplies new telemetry, update the same evidence pack. The next reviewer should see what changed, which bottleneck remained, and why an upgrade still is or is not justified.
